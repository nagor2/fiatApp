**Title:** Why Your Stablecoin Is Losing to Inflation (And What You Can Do About It)

---

If you've been holding USDC, DAI, or USDT over the past few years, you've technically "preserved your capital." Your balance hasn't gone down.

But try buying a tank of gas, a bag of groceries, or a plane ticket with that same amount today compared to three years ago.

You lost. You just didn't see it in your wallet.

## The inflation problem with dollar-pegged stablecoins

All major stablecoins are pegged to the US dollar. That sounds safe — until you remember that the US dollar lost roughly 20% of its purchasing power between 2020 and 2024 due to inflation.

Your USDC didn't lose 20%. Your USDC bought 20% less.

This is the fundamental problem with dollar-pegged stablecoins: they're only "stable" in nominal terms. In real terms — what you can actually buy with them — they're slowly depreciating assets.

For short-term trading, this doesn't matter much. But for savings, for treasury management, for any use case measured in years rather than days, it matters enormously.

## What actually holds value over time?

Historically, real assets — commodities — have maintained purchasing power across decades and centuries.

Gold has been a store of value for thousands of years. Energy commodities track real economic activity. Agricultural commodities track the cost of food. These aren't speculative assets. They're the inputs to the physical economy.

A basket of commodities, by definition, tracks what things actually cost to produce and consume in the real world. If inflation happens, commodity prices generally rise with it. Your purchasing power is preserved.

## Introducing DFC

DFC (DotFlat Coin) is a stablecoin backed by a basket of real commodities — not the dollar.

The mechanics work like this:

1. You deposit ETH as collateral into the DotFlat CDP (Collateralized Debt Position)
2. You receive DFC, overcollateralized against your ETH
3. DFC's value is tracked against a basket of 34 commodities: gold, oil, grains, metals, energy
4. If you want your ETH back, you repay the DFC plus a small fee

It's the same mechanism as MakerDAO's DAI — but instead of tracking USD, it tracks real commodity purchasing power.

## Is it safe?

DotFlat uses the same overcollateralization model that has secured billions of dollars in DAI for years. Your collateral is always worth more than your debt. If the collateral ratio drops too low, the position is liquidated via an on-chain auction — no human intervention, no counterparty risk.

The oracle that provides commodity prices is open-source and permissionless. The contracts are deployed and verified on Ethereum mainnet. There's no team multisig controlling the treasury, no admin key that can mint unlimited tokens.
